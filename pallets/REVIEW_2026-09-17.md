## The blind spot

`SECURITY_AUDIT.md` checked each pallet against its own invariants, and the eight bugs since (D1, D6, D8, §9.6, the I-T1 break, ParamsMismatch, the U256 work, Finding 14) all lived where a value **crosses a boundary and changes meaning**: a balance read by one path and mutated by another (D6), an account derivation that meant one thing at 32 bytes and another at 20 (D8), a constant the mock set small and the runtime set at 18 decimals (D1), an account nothing funds at genesis (§9.6, F14), a shape mirrored off-chain (ParamsMismatch). The mocks model the counterpart pallets' *rules* faithfully — `MockStaking` is a careful transcription of `energy-generation` — but not the *meaning* the counterpart attaches to the same storage.

Everything below has that shape. R1 is a counter one path writes and another path invalidates. R2 is a field one pallet writes as "a trade happened" and another reads as "a human is still interested". R6 is one record read twice in one call. R7 is a bound checked against a constant instead of against the venue's fee. For the auditor: **for every storage item that one path writes and another reads, ask what else moves the thing it describes.**

## Findings

### R1 — HIGH — After a sale, the next `x` LNRG of rewards are attributed to nobody

`LnrgAccounted` is only ever `put(balance)` in `do_harvest`. `do_compound` sells `x` LNRG out of the vault and leaves `LnrgAccounted` where it was. The next harvest computes `delta = balance − accounted`, which is zero until cumulative new rewards exceed `x`; when it finally is positive, `accounted` jumps to the new balance and the first `x` of rewards were never attributed to any launch's shares. They stay in the vault as LNRG that no record owns and no call can reach — there is no sweep for LNRG.

Consequence: every sale forfeits the next equal amount of yield. With a keeper that compounds whenever anything is sellable, the steady state attributes roughly half the yield the stake earns. The spec's I-T2, `LnrgAccounted ≤ balance + Σ sold`, is consistent with the bug — it describes it.

**Observed live** (dev chain, 2026-09-17, after the first compound): `LnrgAccounted = 311,124,066,036`, vault LNRG `43,666,535,585`, launch 5 `lnrg_accrued = 0`: eight eras of payouts in the vault owned by nobody, 49 more eras until `harvest` attributes anything.

**Repro:** stake, pay rewards, harvest, compound (sells all), pay the same rewards again, harvest → `claimable_lnrg` is 0. Test `r1_rewards_after_a_sale_are_attributed_to_nobody`.

**Fix (`df98ad5`):** the sale decrements `LnrgAccounted` by `x`, so it means "attributed LNRG still in the vault", which is what every reader assumed; `try_state` carries `Σ claims ≤ LnrgAccounted ≤ vault LNRG`, which the old code broke on the block of any sale and `t_l7`'s random operations now exercise.

**The eras already unattributed are recoverable, not written off.** The LNRG never left the vault; only the baseline was wrong. Each launch's claim (`lnrg_accrued` + unsettled owed) is tracked correctly, so `Σ claimable` is the right value of `LnrgAccounted` (the old value minus everything ever sold, up to accumulator dust). A chain that ran the old code needs one write — `LnrgAccounted := Σ claimable over Treasuries` — after which the next `harvest` attributes every stranded era to the launches holding shares then. On the fork that is a storage-version migration iterating `Treasuries` (bounded by launch count); on the dev chain, where launch 5 is the only record and its claim is 0, it is `sudo(system.set_storage(LnrgAccounted → 0))`, and the 8-and-counting eras go to TRSL at the next harvest.

### R2 — MEDIUM — The treasury's own buybacks reset the dormancy clock; `retire` is unreachable for any funded launch

`burn_slice` buys through `CurveVenue::buy_for` → `do_buy`, which writes `last_trade_block = now`, and through `PoolManager::swap_for` → `do_swap`, which writes `LastSwapBlock`. `do_retire` reads both as "when a person last traded". A launch with a stake and a keeper never goes `dormancy_blocks` without a "trade": the keeper compounds every interval, each compound with `pending_burn > 0` is a buy. FM-T10 priced a wash trade at 30 bps; the pallet's own trade is free.

Consequence: the retirement path — the only way principal ever returns to a token's holders — is dead in practice, and a launch whose vault has stopped earning (targets chilled, `MaxCooperatorsCount` reached on mainnet, clipped out of the top-128 rewarded cooperators) has its principal locked with no exit at all.

**Repro:** stake, pay rewards, compound once inside the window, no user trade for a full window, `retire` → `NotDormant`. Test `r2_the_treasurys_own_buybacks_reset_the_dormancy_clock`.

**Fix (`db4e717`):** `do_buy` and `do_swap` take `is_trade`; the extrinsics and a settled intent pass true, the venue-trait entry points the treasury uses (`buy_for`, `swap_for`) pass false. `r2_` covers both venues; `l2_` and `d9_` in the launchpad and DEX suites asserted the old rule and now assert this one.

### R3 — LOW — A closed treasury is reopened by the next fee

After a retired treasury burns out, `Treasuries::remove` runs and the pallet can no longer tell "closed" from "never funded": `account_for` answers the vault, the next trade's slice creates a fresh `Active` record, and the token has a treasury again. I-T5 ("Retired never returns to Active") and FM-T9 ("a revived token's slice goes to the protocol forever", which the token page states) both break. Not a loss — arguably a gift to a revived token — but a stated property that does not hold, and the site's history reader (`closedBlock`) assumes it does. Test `r3_a_closed_treasury_is_reopened_by_the_next_fee`.

**Fix (`236bb14`):** the record stays at close, `Retired` with nothing in it; `account_for` already answers `None` for `Retired`. The site's treasury card will show a retired record with zeros where it showed "closed at block N"; the `DustSwept` event still marks the close.

### R4 — LOW (reachability uncertain) — An unquotable `pending_burn` on an active curve launch makes `compound` error instead of no-op

The Retired path sweeps a remainder below ED. The Active path calls `burn_slice` with whatever `pending_burn` holds; on the curve a buy under ~100 wei rounds to nothing after the 1 % fee (`Unquotable`), the error propagates through `?`, and the whole `compound` — including a sale in the same call — reverts. A sale in the same call heals it (the sale adds to `pending_burn` before the burn), so the lasting case is a launch with nothing to sell and dust to burn: `compound` errors at every interval, a keeper whose predicate says "slice due" retries forever.

How dust gets there is the uncertain part: a sale that realises under 100 wei, which needs a 1-wei LNRG remainder (the accumulator leaves those) at a broker rate under 100 VTRS per LNRG (the dev chain's is 188). Test `r4_dust_in_pending_burn_bricks_compound_for_an_active_launch` pins the mechanism at 1 wei.

**Fix (`236bb14`):** `burn_slice` maps the venue's `Unquotable` / `ZeroAmount` to "nothing to burn this call"; the venue refuses before it moves anything, so there is nothing to undo.

### R5 — LOW — One wei sent to the vault fails `try_state` forever

I-T1 is checked as strict equality on an account anyone can transfer to. A 1-wei donation to `0x6d6f646c767472732f6c7074…` makes `do_try_state` fail on every block from then on; nothing accounts for the wei and no call can move it. Anything gated on `try-runtime` (upgrade rehearsals, CI) is a griefing target for the price of a transfer. The same is true of any VTRS that reaches the vault outside `note_fee` (a creator setting `creator_fee_recipient` to the vault, a mis-wired sink). Test `r5_one_wei_sent_to_the_vault_fails_try_state_forever`.

**Fix (`236bb14`):** I-T1 is a floor, `held ≥ expected`. Still open and related: `VaultFunded` withholds ED from the first fee even when the vault already existed (a donation before the first fee), which strands that ED the same way; harmless under the floor.

### R6 — MEDIUM — A solver raising its own bid locks its bond forever

`commit_fill` reads the caller's `Solvers` record at the top, decrements the displaced solver's `active_commitments` from a fresh read, then writes the caller's stale copy back plus one. When displaced and caller are the same solver the decrement is overwritten: the count rises by one per self-overbid and never comes down. `deregister_solver` requires zero, so the 1,000 VTRS bond of a solver who once raised its own bid cannot be refunded. Raising one's bid is the marketplace's intended behaviour under competition. Test `r6_a_solver_raising_its_own_bid_locks_its_bond_forever` (one intent, two commits, `active_commitments == 2`).

**Fix (`8052365`):** a self-replacement leaves the count alone.


### R7 — MEDIUM (modelled, then tested) — Governance can set `max_burn_impact_bps` into a range where a sandwich takes most of every burn slice

FM-T1 accepts the sandwich on the grounds that "the cap bounds what a bracket can extract to a fraction of `max_burn_impact_bps`". That holds only while the slice's price impact is below the venue's **round-trip fee**, because the bracket pays the fee twice and the slice moves the price once. At the default 50 bps it holds on both venues (2 % round trip on the curve, 0.6 % on a 0.3 % pool). `terms_in_bounds` admits up to 500 bps. Modelled on the curve's own constant-product with the fee on both legs (`min_out = 0`, cap read after the front-run):

| venue | cap | slice | best front-run | bracket P&L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pool 0.3 % | 50 bps | 7.5 VTRS | — | 0 |
| pool 0.3 % | 100 bps | 31 VTRS | 3,200 VTRS | **+4.4 VTRS (14 % of the slice)** |
| pool 0.3 % | 200 bps | 94 VTRS | 6,400 VTRS | +45 VTRS (48 %) |
| pool 0.3 % | 500 bps | 235 VTRS | 6,400 VTRS | +171 VTRS (73 %) |
| curve 1 % | ≤ 200 bps | — | — | 0 |
| curve 1 % | 500 bps | 208 VTRS | 6,400 VTRS | +65 VTRS (31 %) |

The bracket needs no timing luck: the bot calls `compound` itself between its two trades, collects the keeper bounty, and the slice executes at whatever price it set. A retiring launch's whole principal goes out this way, one slice per `min_burn_interval`.

**Fix (`0b68065`), bounded in code, not by convention:** refining the model, the bracket breaks even at exactly `2 × fee_bps` on every venue and is profitable one step above. `burn_slice` now reads the venue's fee (`CurveVenue::fee_bps`, `PoolManager::fee_bps`, both new) and sizes the slice at `min(term, 2 × fee − 1)`; the term's range is `[10, 200]`, 200 being twice the highest launch pool tier. `r7_` shows a 200-bps term producing a 59-bps slice on a 0.3 % pool. A governance call can no longer make the default unsafe.

### R8 — MEDIUM (found live, after 222) — A sale that asks for the vault's whole LNRG is refused by the broker

The first `compound` after the R1 recount failed on the dev chain with `Token(NotExpendable)`, twice. The harvest had attributed exactly the vault's whole balance to TRSL (`gross − debt = 76,416,437,273 = balance`: the accumulator's two floors happened to cancel), `sellable` asked the broker for all of it, and the broker — `keep_alive = true`, so `reducible_balance(LNRG, vault, Preserve) ≥ amount_in` — refused, because reducible under `Preserve` is balance minus the asset's `min_balance` of 1. Every earlier sale had worked only because a floor left a wei behind. Data-dependent: the next era's delta may round short and go through; the edge is real and each failing retry costs the keeper a fee. The mock's broker burned the input with `Expendable` and could not see it; it now checks what the real one checks. Test `r8_a_sale_never_asks_for_the_vaults_whole_lnrg`.

Same class as §9.6 and Finding 14: the counterpart pallet's rule about an account's last unit.

**Fix:** `sellable` caps the sale at the vault's reducible LNRG under `Preserve`. The wei that stays is still the launch's (`lnrg_accrued = 1`) and sells with the next accrual.

### R9 — LOW (found by the fuzzer) — A swap of one's whole native balance failed with `CannotCreate`

`do_swap` took the input with `Expendable`: a person swapping every VTRS they had killed their own account, and the non-sufficient launch token then could not be delivered to it — the whole swap reverted with `Token(CannotCreate)`, which nobody can read as "you cannot spend your ED". Reachable, since fees are paid in energy on this chain. The curve's buy and the treasury's own transfers already used `Preserve`. Test `r9_a_swap_of_ones_whole_balance_keeps_the_ed_instead_of_failing`; the fuzzer's minimal case was two steps (a creator's crossing buy, then the 1-VTRS keeper buying with all of it).

**Fix (`design/launch-treasury`):** `do_swap` takes the input's `Preservation` from its caller — `Preserve` for a person's swap and the vault's, `Expendable` for a settled intent, which swaps from the intent escrow and must spend to zero. Spending everything above the ED goes through; the ED itself is refused up front, as on the curve.

### R10 — LOW (found by the fuzzer, 5000 cases) — A swap whose output rounds to zero failed with `BelowMinimum`

1 wei into a pool a 774,000-VTRS buy had nearly drained buys 0.99 of a token unit; `do_swap` computed `amount_out = 0` and went on to transfer it, and pallet-assets refused to open the buyer's token account with nothing in it. `Token(BelowMinimum)`, from the wrong pallet, for "you would get nothing" — and one pallet over, the treasury's burn slice into the same pool errored the same way. Tests `r10_a_swap_that_would_deliver_nothing_says_so`; the fuzzer's minimal cases were six and seven steps.

**Fix (`design/launch-treasury`):** `do_swap` refuses a zero output with `ZeroAmount` before anything moves; `burn_slice` maps the pool venue's `ZeroAmount` to "nothing to burn this call", as it already did the curve's (R4).

### R11 — HIGH (regression from R9; caught by the launchpad's own tests, missed by the fuzzer) — A holder could not sell their whole token position

R9 made a person's swap take its input `Preserve` so a swap of one's whole VTRS balance keeps the ED instead of killing the account. It applied that to whichever asset was the input. pallet-assets reads `Preserve` as "keep the asset's minimum balance", so a swap of one's entire token balance was refused with `Token(NotExpendable)`: after runtime 223 nobody could sell out of a launch token on the pool. `fm03` and `fm13` in `pallets/launchpad/src/tests.rs` went red and were not run before 223 shipped; the fuzzer's `PoolSwap` allowlist accepted any funds error on a sell — "a token-side swap whose output rounds to nothing" — and so accepted this one. Test `r11_selling_ones_whole_token_position_goes_through`.

**Fix (`design/launch-treasury`):** the caller's `Preservation` applies only when the input is the native asset; a token input is always `Expendable`. The fuzz allowlist no longer accepts a funds error on a sell (a sell is clamped to what the seller holds, and a zero output is `ZeroAmount` since R10). Lesson recorded in §Fuzzing: an allowlist arm phrased as a category ("funds") is a hole the size of the category; name the error and the condition.

## Fuzzing, phase one

`pallets/launch-treasury/src/fuzz.rs` (proptest, in `cargo test`; `PROPTEST_CASES` for more). `PROPTEST_CASES=5000 --release` runs both harnesses in about 70 s on this box. In its first 500 cases it reproduced Finding 14 on both legs — from genesis in one step, on the upgrade path in six (a crossing buy, stake, ten blocks, compound: the treasury's burn slice routing 2 wei to a fee escrow nothing funded) — which it allowed by name until F14 was fixed (2026-09-18; allowlist entry now deleted); found R9; at 5000, R10 and its consequence for the burn slice; and confirmed R1, R2 and the ED/rounding edges hold under adversarial ordering. Its allowlist and the two conservation laws are the record of what "expected" means per call.

## Fuzzing, phase two — why it is a replay step, not a fuzzer

Phase two was proposed as "the same Op sequences through the RPC against a chopsticks fork of the
dev chain, the same invariants from storage" — the harness that reaches what the mocks cannot. Before
building it (2026-09-18) every bug on the record was asked: would phase one at 5,000 cases have found
it, given the invariant had been written? Would phase two?

| bug | phase one | phase two | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| R8 — the broker sells with `keep_alive` | **no**: `MockBroker` learned that rule *after* it was seen live | yes | a rule of the counterpart nobody had transcribed |
| D8 — two pools on one 20-byte account | no at the time (the mock was 32-byte); **yes since `b039664`**: the mock is twenty bytes and every derived account is asserted distinct | yes | a truncation that only exists on the real account type |
| R1, R2, R5, §9.6, F14, R9, R10, D6, D1/U256 | yes — it found R9, R10 and F14; R1/R2/R5 once the invariant existed | yes, ~1,000× slower per case | the pallets' own logic; the mocks are faithful enough |
| ParamsMismatch | no | no | an off-chain mirror |

So phase two's unique reach is the R8 class — behaviour of `energy-generation`, `energy-broker`,
`dynamic-energy`, `energy-fee` and `reputation` that no one has transcribed into a mock: reward
arithmetic and clipping under `payout_stakers`, the exchange rate moving every session, the fee layer
moving the broker's depth (a caller short of VNRG buys it with VTRS *through the broker*, before the
call runs), cooperation gates that are consequences of time rather than switches, and real state as
the starting point. It does not add anything about the pallets' own arithmetic and ordering, which
phase one exercises better and far more often.

And it is slow in a way that decides its shape: a chopsticks block with one extrinsic is 0.3–1 s, an
empty block 50–100 ms, an era 100 blocks; a 30-op sequence with the era boundaries harvest and
compound need is 45–90 s. Phase one runs 5,000 cases in about 70 s. Phase two cannot search; it can
only replay what search already found. So:

- **It is a corpus, replayed** — phase one's minimal failing sequences (R9, R10, both F14 legs) and a
  handful of its long random seeds, serialised by `fuzz.rs --dump`, executed as signed extrinsics on
  the fork, with the invariants re-stated over storage — the conservation laws restated as per-op
  balance deltas across named accounts, the fee sink and the broker summing to zero, since on chain VTRS
  leaves as fees and enters the broker as gas purchases.
- **It runs as step two of the upgrade pre-flight** (after the dry-run `setCode`) and **when a
  counterpart changes** — a polkadot-sdk bump or a `develop` merge that touches the five pallets above.
  That is the run that would have caught R8 before 222 shipped: 222's first `compound` was the first ever
  against the real broker.
- **Not on a schedule.** Between those two events nothing it tests has changed; a nightly run is a
  number that is always green.

**The spike (2026-09-18): eras pay the vault on the fork, by authorship, no fallback needed.** A
chopsticks fork of `:9945` in manual-block mode, built across an era boundary (era is 100 blocks here:
`sessionsPerEra = 1`, `epochDuration = 100`), closed the era normally — `ErasEnergyPerStakeCurrency` is
set at era end — and `payout_stakers(validator, closed_era)` rewarded the vault **5,458,316,948
LNRG-wei, exactly the live per-era amount**. The `dev_setStorage`-on-`ErasRewardPoints` fallback is not
needed; the harvest/compound leg is viable on the fork. One number I had wrong: an empty block on this
box is **~2.5–3 s**, not the 50–100 ms I estimated (chopsticks fetches state lazily over the WS
endpoint), so an era boundary is ~4–5 minutes and a corpus of ~15 sequences, half of them crossing a
boundary, is **~30–45 minutes**, not ~15. That is still pre-flight scale and still not searchable — it
sharpens the "replay, not fuzzer" conclusion rather than changing it.

Two of its reaches were cheaper to put into phase one, and were, the same day: twenty-byte accounts
with the derived-accounts-distinct invariant (`b039664`), and a fee layer — every signed op pays
`FEE_VNRG`; short of VNRG the caller buys it with VTRS through `MockBroker`, `keep_alive`, before the
op runs; an unpayable fee means the op never runs — which closes the depth-moves-under-you gap, the
nearest thing phase one has to the R8 class. Both green at 5,000 cases.

The make-or-break for the harvest/compound leg on the fork is whether an era on a chopsticks fork pays
the vault at all (era points come from block authorship, which chopsticks fakes); the fallback is
`dev_setStorage` on `ErasRewardPoints` before the boundary. If neither works, the harvest/compound leg
stays phase-one-only and phase two covers fees, staking and burns.

**Built (2026-09-18).** `vitreus-dex-frontend/scripts/pallet-fuzz-replay` (README there). The corpus is
dumped from `fuzz.rs` (`FUZZ_DUMP=… cargo test … dump_corpus -- --ignored`); the driver replays each
sequence as signed extrinsics on a chopsticks fork of `:9945` and reads the invariants from storage
after every op. The era question resolved in the affirmative — building past a boundary closes the era
and `payout_stakers` rewards the vault the real amount (no `dev_setStorage` fallback needed), so the
harvest/compound leg runs against a real payout: I-T2's LNRG accounting (the R1 class) is checked
against real reward arithmetic. Two invariants are deliberately left to phase one and not re-checked on
the fork — curve k and pool k (single-pallet constant-product arithmetic phase one exercises 5,000×;
re-deriving the rounding in TS only adds false positives) and VTRS summed over all accounts (the fork
has thousands; the per-op rule "issuance rises only across a payout" is the tractable form). `Slash`,
`SetCooperable` and `SetReputation` are mapped but skipped (offence injection / live validator and
reputation state). Six sequences cover the legs and the findings' situations, including R11/R8 (a whole
pool position sold) and R2 (a retirement over eight real eras). Runtime ~30–45 min, which is the point:
a replay step in the upgrade pre-flight, not a searcher and not a nightly job.

## Known and under-rated

- **FM-17, asset-id squatting — FIXED (renumbered from "FM-14" to end the collision with the DEX finding below).** `pallet_assets::create` is `EnsureSigned` on the testnet runtime with `AssetDeposit = 100` wei; creating asset `LaunchAssetBase + NextLaunchId` used to block every `create_launch` with `AssetIdTaken` until a runtime *migration* bumped `NextLaunchId` — a public testnet bricked for the price of nothing, and observed live on the fork (2026-09-18) where the slot was already taken. Fixed in the pallet: `create_launch` walks a monotonic `NextAssetId` cursor to the first free id (bounded by `MAX_ASSET_ID_SCAN`) and uses that; a squatter now only forces the cursor forward at a deposit per id and creation always succeeds. No migration — the cursor defaults to `LaunchAssetBase + NextLaunchId`, so the first create after the upgrade resumes where the old scheme left off and skips an already-squatted slot. `fm17_asset_id_squatting_is_skipped` (red→green).
- **vitreus-dex SECURITY_AUDIT Finding 14 — FIXED** (distinct from FM-17 above). A routed fee slice below the native ED could not create its recipient — the DEX fee escrow before its first fee, or the treasury vault before it was funded — so a swap or curve buy whose slice was under ED failed in full with an unreadable `Token(BelowMinimum)`; the class of §9.6, an account nothing funds at genesis. Fixed both legs: `do_swap` leaves a sub-ED slice in the pool (it accrues to LPs, D7-style) when its recipient has no account, and the launchpad folds a sub-ED curve treasury slice into the protocol share when the vault is absent; a fee-routing total is therefore not exact by that sub-ED amount, stated in code and in the DEX audit. The fee escrow is also funded at setup — a DEX `GenesisConfig` (from-genesis) and the fork migration (upgrade) — so the window never opens on a real chain. Tests `finding14_a_sub_ed_routed_slice_does_not_fail_the_swap` and `finding14_genesis_funds_the_fee_escrow` (red→green); the fuzzer's F14 allowlist entry is deleted and 5000 cases stay green.

## Informational

- **`remove_liquidity`, the last LP.** Reserves include the pool account's native ED; the pool account holds a non-sufficient asset and so cannot fall below ED. The last LP's full removal fails `FundsUnavailable`; they must leave ~ED behind. Cosmetic at 10⁻⁴ VTRS, but the error is unexplained.
- **Yield attribution is by shares at harvest time, not exposure at era time.** A launch that stakes just before a large delayed batch of payouts (a keeper that was down) shares in rewards it was not exposed for. Pro-rata and not extractable (the yield only ever buys the launch's own token), but worth stating in the spec.
- **`MaxCooperatorRewardedPerValidator = 128`, `MaxCooperatorsCount`.** On a busy validator a small vault is clipped out of rewards silently; if the cooperator count is capped the first `cooperate` fails and the vault bonds without earning. Combined with R2 that is principal locked with no yield and no exit. Operational on mainnet; the design knows.
- **Real `energy-generation::unbond` withdraws matured chunks on its own when the ledger's `unlocking` is full** (64). The pallet's `QueueFull` at 64 stops this being reachable, but if `MaxUnlockingChunks` and the pallet's bound ever differ, `finalize_retirement` would see `withdrawn = 0` and credit every matured launch nothing.

## What I checked and did not find

Curve math at the bounds (1-wei buys, crossing rounding: `quote_used ≤ q_in` holds by the ceil/floor pairing); escrow accounting through buy/sell/claim; `sync_reserves` on every DEX entry; seed sweep ordering and provider refs; `bond`/`bond_extra`/`unbond`/`withdraw_unbonded`/`cooperate` against the real pallet (the mock is faithful on every rule the treasury relies on, including the chill-before-unbond and the `min(free − total, extra)` bond); slashing keeps I-T1; `harvest`/`stake`/`retire` ordering within a block; the D9 fold-to-protocol for retiring launches on both legs; `create_launch`'s spare-to-treasury sweep; `settle_intent`'s output accounting under D4 routing.
